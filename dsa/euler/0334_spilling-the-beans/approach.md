# Spilling the Beans - Optimal Approach

## 1. Problem Essence & Formal Mathematical Formulation

In a row of adjacent bowls indexed by integers $\dots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots$, bowls $1, 2, \dots, 1500$ initially contain $b_1, b_2, \dots, b_{1500}$ beans, where $b_i$ are generated by a specified XOR pseudorandom generator.
A valid move consists of choosing a bowl with at least $2$ beans, taking $2$ beans from it, and placing $1$ bean into the immediate left bowl and $1$ bean into the immediate right bowl:

$$
(c(x-1), c(x), c(x+1)) \to (c(x-1) + 1, c(x) - 2, c(x+1) + 1)
$$

The game terminates when no bowl contains $2$ or more beans (i.e. every bowl contains at most $1$ bean).

Find the total number of moves required to reach the final stable state.

---

## 2. The Naive Approach & Fundamental Bottlenecks

### Discrete Event Simulation
A naive simulation implements a worklist queue of active bowls with $c(x) \ge 2$:
- Total beans $N = \sum b_i \approx 1.5 \times 10^6$.
- Total number of moves is $\approx 1.5 \times 10^{17}$.
- Simulating step-by-step at $10^7$ moves/sec would take over $470$ years of CPU runtime.

---

## 3. Core Intuition & Mathematical Structure

### The 1D Chip-Firing Moment Invariants
Under a single move at position $x$:
1. **Zeroth Moment (Total Beans):**

$$
\Delta N = (+1) + (-2) + (+1) = 0 \quad (\text{Conserved})
$$

2. **First Moment (Center of Mass):**

$$
\Delta \mu = (x - 1)(+1) + x(-2) + (x + 1)(+1) = 0 \quad (\text{Conserved})
$$

3. **Second Moment (Variance / Quadratic Invariant):**

$$
\Delta I = (x - 1)^2(+1) + x^2(-2) + (x + 1)^2(+1) = (x^2 - 2x + 1) - 2x^2 + (x^2 + 2x + 1) = \mathbf{+2}
$$

### The Universal Invariant Move Formula:
Every legal chip-firing move increases the second moment $\sum x^2 c(x)$ by **exactly $2$**.
Consequently:

$$
\mathbf{\text{Total Moves} = \frac{I_{\text{final}} - I_{\text{initial}}}{2}}
$$

---

## 4. Rigorous Mathematical Breakthrough & Derivations

### Characterization of the Final Stable State
By the Abelian property of 1D chip-firing (Tardos / Björner-Lovász theorem), the final stable configuration with $N$ chips is unique and consists of $N$ contiguous bowls each containing $1$ chip, with at most **one empty gap** inside the span $[K, K + N]$:
- Span: $N + 1$ consecutive bowls from $x = K$ to $x = K + N$.
- Exactly one bowl $g \in [K, K + N]$ is empty ($c(g) = 0$), while all other $N$ bowls contain $c(x) = 1$.

### Determining $K$ and $g$ via First Moment Conservation:
The first moment of this configuration is:

$$
\mu = \sum_{x=K}^{K+N} x - g = (N + 1) K + \frac{N(N + 1)}{2} - g
$$

Since $K \le g \le K + N$:

$$
K = \left\lfloor \frac{\mu - N(N - 1)/2}{N} \right\rfloor
$$

$$
g = (N + 1) K + \frac{N(N + 1)}{2} - \mu
$$

The final second moment is computed in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ via sum-of-squares:

$$
I_{\text{final}} = \sum_{x=K}^{K+N} x^2 - g^2
$$

## 5. Concrete Step-by-Step Example Walkthrough

### Verification on 3 Bowls with $b = [1, 2, 3]$:
1. $N = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6$.
2. $\mu = 0(1) + 1(2) + 2(3) = 8$.
3. $I_{\text{initial}} = 0^2(1) + 1^2(2) + 2^2(3) = 14$.
4. $K = \lfloor (8 - 6(5)/2) / 6 \rfloor = \lfloor -7 / 6 \rfloor = -2$.
5. $g = 7(-2) + 21 - 8 = -1$.
6. Final state span $[-2, 4]$ with gap at $g = -1$: bowls $\{-2, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ each have $1$ bean.
7. $I_{\text{final}} = ((-2)^2 + 0^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2 + 4^2) = 4 + 0 + 1 + 4 + 9 + 16 = 34$.
8. $\text{Moves} = (34 - 14) / 2 = \mathbf{10}$. (Matches simulation! $\checkmark$)

## 6. Implementation Architecture & Algorithmic Blueprint

### Algorithmic Execution Pipeline

| Stage | Operation | Formula Action | Complexity |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Stage 1** | **PRNG Generation** | Generate $b_1 \dots b_{1500}$ | $\mathcal{O}(\text{bowls})$ |
| **Stage 2** | **Initial Moments** | Compute $N = \sum b_i, \mu = \sum i b_i, I_{\text{init}} = \sum i^2 b_i$ | $\mathcal{O}(\text{bowls})$ |
| **Stage 3** | **Boundary & Gap** | $K = \lfloor (\mu - N(N-1)/2) / N \rfloor, \quad g = (N+1)K + N(N+1)/2 - \mu$ | $\mathcal{O}(1)$ |
| **Stage 4** | **Move Count** | $\text{Moves} = (\sum_{x=K}^{K+N} x^2 - g^2 - I_{\text{init}}) / 2$ | $\mathcal{O}(1)$ |

### Critical Invariants & Edge Cases Handled:
1. **Confluent Uniqueness:** The Abelian property guarantees the total move count is independent of the sequence of moves.
2. **Negative Coordinates:** The closed-form square sum handles negative span boundaries $[K, K + N]$.
3. **Exact Integer Arithmetic:** Python handles large integers $\approx 1.5 \times 10^{17}$ with exact precision.
