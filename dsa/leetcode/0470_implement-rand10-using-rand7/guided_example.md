# Guided Example: Implement Rand10() Using Rand7()

We trace the step-by-step $7 \times 7$ 2D grid flattening ($x = (r_1 - 1) \times 7 + r_2 \in [1, 49]$), Rejection Sampling thresholding ($x \le 40$), uniform 10-bucket partitioning ($(x - 1) \pmod{10} + 1$), expected call analysis ($E[\text{calls}] = 2.45$), and geometric distribution convergence on representative sampling runs:

- **Input:** API `rand7()` generating uniform integers in $[1, 7]$
- **Required output:** Uniform random integers in $[1, 10]$ ($P(k) = \frac{1}{10}$ for all $k \in [1, 10]$)
- **Rejection sampling execution trace:**
  - **Grid Construction ($7 \times 7$ Outcomes):**
    - Draw two independent numbers: $r_1 = rand7(), \; r_2 = rand7()$
    - Map $(r_1, r_2)$ to a 1D scalar index:
      $$
      x = (r_1 - 1) \times 7 + r_2
      $$
    - Because each pair $(r_1, r_2)$ is drawn independently with uniform probability $\frac{1}{7} \times \frac{1}{7} = \frac{1}{49}$:
      Every integer $x \in [1, 49]$ occurs with exact probability $\frac{1}{49}$.
  - **Threshold Selection:**
    - We need to partition into $10$ equal groups.
    - The largest multiple of $10$ that is $\le 49$ is **$40$**.
    - **Acceptance Region ($x \le 40$):**
      - Exactly $40$ numbers are accepted.
      - Map $x$ to $[1, 10]$: $(x - 1) \pmod{10} + 1$.
      - Each number $k \in [1, 10]$ is generated by exactly $4$ values of $x$:
        - Outcome 1: $\{1, 11, 21, 31\}$
        - Outcome 2: $\{2, 12, 22, 32\}$
        - ...
        - Outcome 10: $\{10, 20, 30, 40\}$
      - Conditional probability for each outcome:
        $$
        P(k \mid x \le 40) = \frac{4 / 49}{40 / 49} = \frac{4}{40} = \frac{1}{10}
        $$
    - **Rejection Region ($x > 40$):**
      - Values $41, 42, \dots, 49$ (9 out of 49 outcomes) are rejected.
      - Discard and draw a new pair $(r_1, r_2)$.
- **Worked Run 1 (Accepted on first attempt):**
  - Draw $r_1 = 1, \; r_2 = 1$
  - Index: $x = (1 - 1) \times 7 + 1 = \mathbf{1}$
  - Check $x \le 40$: $1 \le 40$ (Accepted!)
  - Emitted integer: $(1 - 1) \pmod{10} + 1 = 0 + 1 = \mathbf{1}$
- **Worked Run 2 (Rejection before acceptance):**
  - Attempt 1: Draw $r_1 = 7, \; r_2 = 7 \implies x = 6 \times 7 + 7 = \mathbf{49}$
    - $49 > 40 \implies$ **Reject!** Draw again.
  - Attempt 2: Draw $r_1 = 1, \; r_2 = 2 \implies x = 0 \times 7 + 2 = \mathbf{2}$
    - $2 \le 40 \implies$ **Accepted!**
    - Emitted integer: $(2 - 1) \pmod{10} + 1 = 1 + 1 = \mathbf{2}$

This instance demonstrates rejection sampling from non-divisible uniform distributions, mathematically proves why discarding biased tails guarantees exact uniformity, and derives $O(1)$ expected runtime and $O(1)$ space bounds.

## 1. Instance & Teaching Goal

Given a black-box random number generator `rand7()` that returns a uniformly distributed integer in the range $[1, 7]$:
Implement `rand10()` to generate a uniformly distributed integer in $[1, 10]$:
$$
P(X = k) = \frac{1}{10} \quad \forall k \in \{1, 2, \dots, 10\}
$$
You cannot call any other random number generator.

```text
7 x 7 Cartesian Grid (49 cells):
       c1  c2  c3  c4  c5  c6  c7
  r1 [  1   2   3   4   5   6   7 ]
  r2 [  8   9  10  11  12  13  14 ]
  r3 [ 15  16  17  18  19  20  21 ]
  r4 [ 22  23  24  25  26  27  28 ]
  r5 [ 29  30  31  32  33  34  35 ]
  r6 [ 36  37  38  39  40 |41  42 ]
  r7 [ 43  44  45  46  47  48  49 ]
                          ^-------^
                   Rejected cells (41-49)

Accepted range [1, 40]: exactly 4 occurrences per digit 1..10
```

### The Incommensurability Problem
- A single call to `rand7()` produces 7 outcomes. Since 7 is not divisible by 10 (and $\gcd(7, 10) = 1$), no deterministic mapping from a single roll can produce a uniform distribution over 10 values without bias.
- Calling `rand7()` twice yields $7 \times 7 = 49$ independent outcomes.
- While 49 is still not divisible by 10, the largest multiple of 10 within 49 is **40**.
- By using **Rejection Sampling**, we accept outcomes in $[1, 40]$ and redraw whenever an outcome falls into $[41, 49]$.

## 2. Conceptual Foundation & Invariants

### 1. 2D Coordinate Flattening:
Let $r_1$ and $r_2$ be two independent draws from `rand7()`:
$$
x = (r_1 - 1) \times 7 + r_2
$$
- $r_1 - 1 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$ (row index).
- $r_2 \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7\}$ (column offset).
- $x$ spans the integers $1$ through $49$ bijectively.
- Because $P(r_1 = a, r_2 = b) = \frac{1}{7} \times \frac{1}{7} = \frac{1}{49}$ for all $(a, b)$, every integer in $[1, 49]$ is generated with equal probability $\frac{1}{49}$.

### 2. The Rejection Sampling Principle:
- If $x \le 40$:
  - Accept $x$ and return:
    $$
    result = ((x - 1) \pmod{10}) + 1
    $$
  - The map $x \mapsto ((x - 1) \pmod{10}) + 1$ maps exactly 4 distinct values of $x$ to each output integer $k \in [1, 10]$:
    $$
    k \longleftarrow \{k, \; k + 10, \; k + 20, \; k + 30\}
    $$
  - Probability of returning $k$:
    $$
    P(k) = \frac{4 \times \frac{1}{49}}{40 \times \frac{1}{49}} = \frac{4}{40} = \frac{1}{10}
    $$
- If $x > 40$:
  - Reject $x$. Discard the draw and sample again.

> **Uniformity Theorem.** Under rejection sampling on a discrete uniform space, the conditional distribution of any accepted subset is strictly uniform on that subset.

## 3. Step-by-Step Worked Execution

We trace two concrete sampling trials:

---

### Trial 1: First-Pass Acceptance
- Draw 1: $r_1 = 1$.
- Draw 2: $r_2 = 1$.
- Compute 1D coordinate:
  $$
  x = (1 - 1) \times 7 + 1 = 0 + 1 = \mathbf{1}
  $$
- Threshold check:
  $$
  x \le 40 \iff 1 \le 40 \quad (\mathbf{Accepted!})
  $$
- Map to $[1, 10]$:
  $$
  ((1 - 1) \pmod{10}) + 1 = (0 \pmod{10}) + 1 = \mathbf{1}
  $$
- Calls used: 2 calls to `rand7()`.

---

### Trial 2: Rejection and Retry
- **Attempt 1:**
  - Draw 1: $r_1 = 7$.
  - Draw 2: $r_2 = 7$.
  - Coordinate:
    $$
    x = (7 - 1) \times 7 + 7 = 42 + 7 = \mathbf{49}
    $$
  - Threshold check:
    $$
    49 \le 40 \quad (\mathbf{False: Reject!})
    $$
  - Discard $49$ and retry.
- **Attempt 2:**
  - Draw 3: $r_1 = 1$.
  - Draw 4: $r_2 = 2$.
  - Coordinate:
    $$
    x = (1 - 1) \times 7 + 2 = 0 + 2 = \mathbf{2}
    $$
  - Threshold check:
    $$
    2 \le 40 \quad (\mathbf{Accepted!})
    $$
  - Map to $[1, 10]$:
    $$
    ((2 - 1) \pmod{10}) + 1 = (1 \pmod{10}) + 1 = \mathbf{2}
    $$
- Total calls used: 4 calls to `rand7()`.

## 5. Boundary Cases & Failure Modes

- **Upper Accepted Boundary ($x = 40$):** $r_1 = 6, r_2 = 5 \implies x = (5 \times 7) + 5 = 40$. $40 \le 40 \implies ((39) \% 10) + 1 = \mathbf{10}$.
- **Lower Accepted Boundary ($x = 1$):** $r_1 = 1, r_2 = 1 \implies x = 1 \implies ((0) \% 10) + 1 = \mathbf{1}$.
- **First Rejected Value ($x = 41$):** $r_1 = 6, r_2 = 6 \implies x = 41 > 40 \implies$ Redraws.
- **Maximum Cell ($x = 49$):** $r_1 = 7, r_2 = 7 \implies x = 49 > 40 \implies$ Redraws.

---

## 6. Traps & Common Anti-Patterns

- **Using Addition Without Multiplication ($r_1 + r_2$):** Adding two dice $r_1 + r_2$ produces a triangular distribution with peak at 8 (like 2d6 in Craps), destroying uniformity. Generating uniform numbers requires the 2D Cartesian grid formula $(r_1 - 1) \times 7 + r_2$.
- **Not Rejecting Biased Tails:** Mapping all 49 numbers via $x \pmod{10}$ means numbers $1 \dots 9$ appear 5 times while number 10 appears only 4 times ($P(1) = 5/49 \ne P(10) = 4/49$), introducing statistical bias. Rejection sampling of the 9 excess cells is mandatory for strict mathematical uniformity.
- **Using Floating-Point Approximations:** Converting to floats with `rand7() / 7.0` introduces IEEE 754 floating-point rounding errors and non-uniform binary mantissa binning. Pure integer rejection is exact.

## 7. Complexity Derivation

- **Time Complexity:**
  - The probability of accepting in any given round is:
    $$
    p = \frac{40}{49} \approx 0.8163 \quad (81.63\%)
    $$
  - The number of iterations follows a Geometric distribution $\text{Geom}(p)$.
  - Expected number of iterations:
    $$
    E[\text{iterations}] = \frac{1}{p} = \frac{49}{40} = 1.225
    $$
  - Expected calls to `rand7()` per generated integer:
    $$
    E[\text{calls}] = 2 \times 1.225 = \mathbf{2.45}
    $$
  - Worst-case time is theoretically unbounded, but the probability of needing $\ge 10$ attempts is $(9/49)^{10} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-8}$.
  - Expected Time: $\mathcal{O}(1)$.
- **Auxiliary Space Complexity:**
  - $\mathcal{O}(1)$ space using two scalar variables.
