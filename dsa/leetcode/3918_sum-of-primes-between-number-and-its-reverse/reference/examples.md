## Examples

**Example 1**

- Input: `n = 13`
- Output: `132`
- **Explanation:** Reversing `13` produces `31`, so the inclusive interval is `[13, 31]`. Its primes are `13`, `17`, `19`, `23`, `29`, and `31`, whose sum is `13 + 17 + 19 + 23 + 29 + 31 = 132`.

**Example 2**

- Input: `n = 10`
- Output: `17`
- **Explanation:** The reversal is `1`, making the interval `[1, 10]`. The primes `2`, `3`, `5`, and `7` sum to `17`.

**Example 3**

- Input: `n = 8`
- Output: `0`
- **Explanation:** Reversing one digit leaves `8`, so the interval is `[8, 8]`. Since `8` is not prime, the interval contributes no prime and the result is `0`.
