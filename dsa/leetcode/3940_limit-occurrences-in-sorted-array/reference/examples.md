## Examples

**Example 1**

- Input: `nums = [1,1,1,2,2,3], k = 2`
- Output: `[1,1,2,2,3]`
- Explanation: At most two copies of each value may remain. The three copies of `1` are reduced to two, both copies of `2` are retained, and the single `3` is retained. Combining those groups in their original order gives `[1,1,2,2,3]`.

**Example 2**

- Input: `nums = [1,2,3], k = 1`
- Output: `[1,2,3]`
- Explanation: Every value is already distinct, so each appears no more than once and the array remains unchanged.
